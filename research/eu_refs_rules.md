### Candidates

**A. The AI Act trilogue, Brussels, 6–8 December 2023 (pick).**
- The final trilogue ran 22 hours from Wednesday into Thursday morning and ended "with only a deal to resume talks the next day".
- "Exhausted negotiators then restarted talks at 0800 GMT on Friday." The whole thing is described as "36 hours of negotiations". (AFP via France 24)
- The deal came "a little before midnight Brussels' time" on 8 Dec, after talks "which stretched to almost three days". A negotiator called it "a marathon negotiation by all standards, looking at all precedents so far". (TechCrunch)
- IAPP confirms the 22-hour session and Breton's "resume work the next day at 9:00 CET". The EP Legislative Train dates the provisional agreement 9 Dec 2023.
- It is literally a Chinese room: a fixed rulebook, texts passed in and out, and outsiders who cannot see inside.
- Sources:
  - https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20231208-eu-strikes-deal-on-landmark-ai-law
  - https://techcrunch.com/2023/12/08/eu-ai-act-political-deal/

**B. Parliament's interpreting booths as Searle's room.**
- "With 24 official languages, there are 552 possible language combinations."
- Parliament sometimes uses "relay" languages: a speech goes first into English, French or German, "and then into others".
- So a Maltese speech can reach Finnish through a booth where the interpreter works from English, not Maltese: Searle's symbols-under-the-door set-up (1980, *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*), run in shifts.
- Sources:
  - https://www.europarl.europa.eu/news/en/faq/0/which-languages-are-in-use-in-the-parliament
  - https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/chinese-room/

## L10 "where the faxes zoom"

**Timing (estimated, see the note at the top):**
- Words: where ≈27.94, the ≈28.52, **faxes ≈28.87**, **zoom ≈29.45**–29.91. Machine transcription of the take heard "Where the fax is so See" (`audio/el_music/eu_final_report.json`).
- Beats: 27.96, 28.42, 28.87, **29.33 (downbeat)**, 29.78.

### Candidates

**A. Dürer's Rhinoceros as a fax from 1515 (pick; keeps the existing asset, retold).**
- An Indian rhinoceros reached Lisbon on 20 May 1515.
- Valentim Fernandes wrote a newsletter to the Nuremberg merchants in June 1515. "The original document in German has not survived, but a transcript in Italian is held in the Biblioteca Nazionale Centrale Firenze." A second, anonymous letter enclosed a sketch.
- Dürer worked from the letter and the sketch and "never saw the actual rhinoceros".
- The woodcut's own inscription:
  - It says "This is an accurate representation".
  - It gets the date wrong: "the first of May in the year 1513".
  - It ends: "They also say that the rhinoceros is fast, merry and jovial." That is the "zoom".
- It adds "a small twisted horn on its back", and German school textbooks used it as a faithful image until "the late 1930s".
- The retelling: a picture sent by letter across Europe and rebuilt at the other end, with confident errors, is a lossy fax.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCrer%27s_Rhinoceros

**B. Caselli's pantelegraph, the first commercial fax: Paris–Lyon, 1865.**
- **The sibling research already took this for L22** ("One E thirty faxes a second"; `research/eu_refs_society.md`, with `caselli-pantelegraph.jpg` and `caselli-writing-sample.jpg` already in `app/public/animatic/img/`). Use it here only if L22 moves off it. L22's rate joke is the better home.
- It was "in full-scale operation by 1865 between Paris and Lyon" and "extended to Marseille in 1867", and it ran until Sedan (1870).
- It "transmitted almost 5,000 faxes" in its first year, at a peak rate of "110 per hour". A test sent Rossini's signature from Paris to Amiens (140 km), and banks used the line to check signatures.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Caselli
  - PD plates: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:T2-_d160_-_Fig._73._%E2%80%94_Le_pant%C3%A9l%C3%A9graphe_Caselli.png (2740×2721)
  - https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:T2-_d163_-_Fig._75._%E2%80%94_Exemple_d%E2%80%99%C3%A9criture_du_pant%C3%A9l%C3%A9graphe_Caselli.png (1767×761). This is a real 1867 fax in scan lines. Even if the plate is not used here, it is the texture reference for step 3 below.

**C. The fax today, as a caption line only.**
- 77% of German companies still fax (Bitkom press release, 17 Jun 2024; 604 firms surveyed).
- Alexander Bain's patent of 27 May 1843 already claimed that "a copy of any other surface … can be taken".
- Sources:
  - https://www.bitkom.org/Presse/Presseinformation/3-von-4-Bueros-gefaxt
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Bain_(inventor)

### Candidates

**A. Hansel and Gretel, Brothers Grimm, *Kinder- und Hausmärchen* no. 15, 1812 (pick).**
- The house "war ganz aus Brod gebaut und war mit Kuchen gedeckt, und die Fenster waren von hellem Zucker".
- Every day the witch says: „Hänsel, streck deine Finger heraus, daß ich fühle, ob du bald fett genug bist.“
- „Hänsel streckte ihr aber immer ein Knöchlein heraus“, and she wondered why he never gained weight. Later in the tale: „meine Augen sind schwach, ich kann nicht so weit sehen.“
- This is the whole cookie economy in one European tale: a house made of cake that you cannot leave, daily tracking by touch, and the first anti-fingerprinting measure (a bone).
- The word itself is European too: "cookie" comes from Dutch *koekje*, "little cake", attested in American English by 1808.
- Sources:
  - https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/H%C3%A4nsel_und_Gretel_(1812)

**B. Planet49, Court of Justice (Grand Chamber), C-673/17, Luxembourg, 1 October 2019.**
- A German online lottery pre-ticked the cookie box.
- The operative part: consent "is not validly constituted if … the storage of information or access to information already stored in a website user's terminal equipment is permitted by way of a pre-checked checkbox which the user must deselect to refuse his or her consent".
- The lottery was free to enter, which gives "please let me free" a second reading.
- Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62017CJ0673

**C. The "cookie law": Directive 2009/136/EC of 25 November 2009,** which rewrote Art. 5(3) of the ePrivacy Directive. The existing "1,029 partners / 368 legitimate interest" figures come from `research/zeitgeist.md` (IAB vendor list v178).
- Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2009/136/oj/eng

**Timing:**
- Words: **Now 77.72**, **private 78.16**–78.82, **chats 78.82**–80.04, **obsolete 80.04**–81.21.

**Problem with the current shot:** the director called the chat-bubble "SCANNED" version bad content. The story below has no chat bubbles and no SCANNED stamp. The subject is the one thing everyone has done: a sealed private letter, and whether the state opened it.

### Candidates

**A. Mazzini's poppy-seed test, London, 1844 (pick).**
- The Italian exile put poppy seeds and grains of sand in his letters. "When they arrived empty, he caused the MP Thomas Duncombe to raise a complaint in Parliament." (David Vincent, History & Policy)
- On 14 June 1844 Duncombe presented a petition from "W. J. Linton, Joseph Mazzini, and two others" that "their seals had been broken, and that they had been opened and read". Home Secretary Sir James Graham admitted "a warrant had been issued". (Hansard)
- Punch drew Graham as "Paul Pry at the Post Office" and sold "Anti-Graham Wafers" for sealing letters. The Post Office's "Secret" branch was abolished in August 1844. (Marjorie Stone, BRANCH)
- The link to now comes from the EDPB–EDPS Joint Opinion 04/2022 (28 July 2022) on COM(2022) 209. It says client-side scanning "would likely lead to substantial, untargeted access and processing of unencrypted content on end user's devices" (para. 100).
  - Mazzini's test works only if the letter is opened after sealing.
  - Scanning on the device reads it before the seal, so the seeds would still be there.
- Sources:
  - https://historyandpolicy.org/policy-papers/papers/surveillance-privacy-and-history/
  - https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1844/jun/14/opening-letters-post-office
  - https://branchcollective.org/?ps_articles=marjorie-stone-on-the-post-office-espionage-scandal-1844
  - https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2022-07/edpb_edps_jointopinion_202204_csam_en_0.pdf

**B. Stasi Department M (Abteilung M), GDR, 1950–1989.**
- Staff in the MfS post rooms ("Stelle 12") in the GDR's 15 mail-sorting offices pulled letters out. The letters were steamed open with hot or cold steam.
- From the 1970s there were "Brieföffnungsautomaten, Briefschließmaschinen und … Röntgentechnik" (letter-opening machines, letter-sealing machines and X-ray equipment).
- In October 1989 the line had 2,192 officers. Between January 1984 and November 1989 it took 32.8 million DM out of letters.
- A staff member ironed resealed letters flat: „Eine MfS-Mitarbeiterin bügelt wieder verschlossene Briefe“.
- Meanwhile Art. 31(1) of the 1968 GDR constitution read: „Post- und Fernmeldegeheimnis sind unverletzbar.“
- This version is strong, but there is no PD/CC0 image (Stasi archive photos are not PD), so every prop would have to be drawn.
- Sources:
  - https://www.bundesarchiv.de/im-archiv-recherchieren/stasi-unterlagen-einsehen/hinweise-zum-mfs/mfs-lexikon/detail/abteilung-m-postkontrolle/
  - https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutsche-teilung/stasi/223937/allwissenheit-als-ziel-die-postkontrolle-der-ddr-geheimpolizei/
  - https://www.demokratie-statt-diktatur.de/stasi-und-die-menschenrechte/postgeheimnis/
  - https://www.verfassungen.de/ddr/verf68.htm

**C. Vienna's Geheime Kabinettskanzlei (1711–1848).**
- It was set up in 1711 in the Stallburg and dissolved in 1848.
- Embassy mail arrived at 7 am. Secretaries melted the seals while stenographers copied, and "within three hours the letters had been resealed and returned to the central post office".
- This gives a clock-driven beat: 7:00 → 10:00 in one line.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_noir
  - https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geheime_Kabinettskanzlei

### Candidates

**A. Ellis Island: the diagnosis chalked on your coat (pick only if L26 means leaving Europe).**
- "Over 12 million immigrants were processed at Ellis Island during the peak years of 1892–1924." (NPS)
- E. H. Mullan wrote in *Public Health Reports* (vol. 32, no. 20, 18 May 1917) that the line officers chalked marks "on the anterior aspect of the right shoulder".
  - The codes: "B … [back]; C, conjunctivitis; CT, trachoma; E, eyes; F, face; Ft, feet; G, goiter; H, heart; K, hernia; L, lameness; N, neck; P, physical and lungs; Pg, pregnancy; Sc, scalp; S, senility". X meant a suspected mental defect, and a circled X a definite one.
  - "15 to 20 percent of the immigrants are chalk-marked."
- This is health data written on your back in public, in six seconds, with no consent: the world without a GDPR, seen by a European arriving there.
- Sources:
  - https://www.nps.gov/elis/index.htm
  - https://www.ggarchives.com/Immigration/Inspection/MentalExaminationAndLineInspectionAtEllisIsland-1917.html (a transcription of Mullan, 1917)

**B. The GDPR's twin that never arrived: the ePrivacy Regulation.** This option keeps the DELAY meter.
- It was proposed on 10 Jan 2017 (COM(2017) 10) and "was intended to come in effect on 25 May 2018, together with the GDPR".
- The Council mandate came on 10 Feb 2021. The Commission withdrew it in the 2025 work programme, and the withdrawal was published in the OJ on 6 Oct 2025.
- So the GDPR stayed single, and the delay was 8 years 9 months, near enough to the treatment's `+9 YEARS`. This could reuse the BER flap-board code as-is: `ePRIVACY REG. · DUE 25.05.2018 WITH GDPR` → 2019 … 2025 → `WITHDRAWN`.
- It also ties together three lines: the cookie law in L17 and the Chat Control derogation in L25 both sit on the ePrivacy Directive.
- Sources:
  - https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-connected-digital-single-market/file-jd-e-privacy-reform
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EPrivacy_Regulation

**C. Europe's side of the ledger: the census, from Bruegel to Karlsruhe (pick, given the committed L26).**
- Bruegel's *Census at Bethlehem* (1566; now in Brussels) is a registration of new arrivals. Among the people heading for the inn are "Joseph and the pregnant Virgin Mary on a donkey".
- At the inn "a group of people is gathered … having their details taken down by a scribe", under "a sign bearing the Habsburg double-headed eagle".
