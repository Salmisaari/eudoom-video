## The mill

Knut Fredrik Idestam (28 Oct 1838 – 8 Apr 1916), a mining engineer, got a Senate permit on 12 May 1865 to build a groundwood mill at the lower falls of the Tammerkoski rapids in Tampere. Nokia treats that date as its founding. The mill started early in 1866 and made groundwood pulp, wood ground for paper, using Heinrich Voelter's machines from Germany. It was the first commercially successful groundwood mill in Finland, not the first attempt. Source: Tapio Helen, "Fredrik Idestam", National Biography of Finland, https://kansallisbiografia.fi/english/person/4296

A second mill was built at Nokia in 1868, fifteen kilometres west of Tampere (same biography). In 1871 Idestam and Leo Mechelin founded Nokia Ab and the business took the river's name. Nokia's own archived history says the same: a wood-pulp mill on the Tammerkoski in 1865, a second mill by the Nokianvirta, the company named Nokia Ab in 1871. https://web.archive.org/web/20090217164929/http://www.nokia.com/about-nokia/company/story-of-nokia/nokias-first-century/the-birth-of-nokia

The plate in `app/public/animatic/img/nokia-paper-mill.jpg` is captioned "Nokia Paper-Mill" in Mechelin's *Finland in the Nineteenth Century* (1894), p. 201. It shows the later works at Nokia, with a tall stack, sheds and the river, not the 1865 Tampere mill. Public domain. The Commons portrait of Idestam has no license template on the file page, so it was not downloaded.

## The moon

On 14 Oct 2020 NASA selected Nokia of America Corporation, Sunnyvale, for $14.1 million under the fifth Tipping Point solicitation, to deploy the first LTE/4G communications system in space. https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-announces-partners-to-advance-tipping-point-technologies-for-the-moon-mars/ and https://www.nasa.gov/technology/2020-nasa-tipping-point-selections/

IM-2 launched at 6:16 p.m. on 26 Feb 2025 on a Falcon 9 from Kennedy LC-39A. Athena touched down at about 11:30 a.m. CST on 6 March 2025, on its side inside a crater, more than 1,300 feet (400 meters) from Mons Mouton. The same NASA release's photo caption says about 820 feet. The mission ended at 12:15 a.m. CST on 7 March. https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-receives-some-data-before-intuitive-machines-ends-lunar-mission/

Nokia's 10 March 2025 release: the Lunar Surface Communications System's network-in-a-box powered on, reached an on-air state (base station, radio and core) and ran for a 25-minute power window, exchanging commands with Nokia mission control in Sunnyvale. No cellular call was made. The device on the Micro Nova hopper had dropped below its operating temperature by the time the network came up, so no connection was established. Cause, per Nokia: the lander's solar panels were badly oriented after the landing. https://www.nokia.com/newsroom/nokia-and-intuitive-machines-deliver-first-cellular-network-to-the-moon-achieve-some-key-mission-objectives/

NASA's 7 March release is narrower: Nokia's 4G/LTE payload "only able to complete some objectives," with some checkouts in flight and on the surface.
